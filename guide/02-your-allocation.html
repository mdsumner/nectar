2. Your allocation

Status: draft

How to get your own Nectar resources: the Project Trial you get at first login, then applying for a bigger allocation when you need one.

If your group already runs a Nectar project, its manager can add you as a member instead; ask them how they do it.

Logging in

  • Go to the dashboard and choose Login via AAF (Australia) or Login via Tuakiri (New Zealand), then use your university credentials (logging in).
  • Not sure you’re eligible? Use the eligibility checker (about). If your organisation isn’t in AAF, a collaborator’s institution or an AAF Virtual Home account may work (no AAF).
  • Your account goes inactive after 6 months without a login; logging in again reactivates it.

Your Project Trial

Your first login creates a Project Trial (pt-NNNN): up to 4 vCPU, expiring after 6 months, and not shareable (Project Trial, tutorial). The tutorial describes the budget as “4 vCPU for up to 6 vCPU-months”. A shut-off instance still counts; only shelving or deleting stops it. A trial is fine for learning how to launch a VM (04-launch-a-vm.md), but it has no DNS name and is too small for most real work.

Applying

  1. Link your ORCID first; the form won’t go through without it (ORCID).

  2. In the dashboard, Allocations > New Request. You can convert your Project Trial into the new project (it keeps whatever you’ve built, and you get a fresh empty trial) or start a new project (converting).

  3. Choose a Resource Bundle (bundles):

    Bundle Service Units per year
    Standard 4,000
    Powerful 8,000
    Extreme 16,000
    Custom what you justify

    Standard also includes up to 40 instances, 64 vCPU, 128 GB RAM, 500 GB of object storage, 4 routers and 8 floating IPs (bundle contents). SU are spent per hour by each running (or shut-off) instance and don’t carry over (Service Units). 03-sizing.md shows how to turn “the machine I need” into SU.

  4. Volumes are requested separately, as “Location Specific Resources”, per availability zone (e.g. 500 GB in tasmania). Ask for them in the zone where you’ll run your instance; see 05-storage.md.

  5. Describe the research, funding and why you need these resources. Track the request under Allocations > My Requests.

Review is done by the local node or the national Allocation Committee and “can take up to four weeks”; bigger requests need more justification (Project Trial and allocations).

National or local?

(national vs local)

  • National: approved by the Allocation Committee, and you can run in any availability zone. You need national or international competitive grant funding, NCRIS/ARDC funding, or government/industry funding (policy).
  • Local: at a node’s discretion, for researchers at that node’s member institutions, and resources are at that node only.

Current nodes (node list, Aug 2026): Intersect, Queensland (UQ), Adelaide University, Tasmania (UTAS), Melbourne Research Cloud, Monash, Swinburne, Pawsey, and National (ARDC-AARNet). Auckland is a private node for the University of Auckland only. ANU, Deakin, USyd, RMIT and Macquarie are listed as unaffiliated. Pawsey’s old Nimbus cloud closed on 31 March 2025; its Nectar node went live in December 2025. Renewing and amending

Allocations last up to 12 months (allocations). To renew, or to ask for more, use Allocations > My Requests > Amend/Extend allocation (tutorial). Submitting an amendment pauses the expiry process below.

What happens at expiry

An allocation expires at its end date, or when it has used 100% of its SU (expiry):

When What happens
4 weeks before, or at 80% of SU Warning email
Expiry Quota drops to zero; running instances keep running
+ 2 weeks Instances stopped and locked
+ 30 days Instances snapshotted, then terminated
+ 3 months Everything deleted (except public objects and shared snapshots)

Don’t rely on this as a safety net: copy your data off before the end.

What to put in your request

You’ll be asked how much compute and storage you need. Work it out before you start the form:

  • which flavour (cores and RAM), how many instances, and for how many months, which together give your Service Units;
  • how much volume storage, and in which availability zone;
  • how much object storage, if any.

03-sizing.md has the commands to measure what you use now, and worked examples of the SU arithmetic.
